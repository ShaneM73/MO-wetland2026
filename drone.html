What the layers show

  • Orthophoto: colour mosaic from the drone camera, 3 cm pixels, early spring before leaf-out.
  • Drone surface relief: the LiDAR surface model (tops of whatever the pulses hit: ground in the open fen, tree crowns in the woods), shaded and coloured by elevation.
  • Height above bare earth: drone surface minus the USGS 1 m bare-earth DEM, at 25 cm. Pale = within 0.5 m of the ground (open fen, meadow); green = shrubs and trees; grey = surface more than 1 m below the USGS ground, mostly artefacts along the flight edge.
  • USGS 1 m bare earth: the 3DEP DEM the watershed model uses, same colours, for comparison of detail.

Caveats

  • The files are marked Preliminary. Elevations were delivered about 26.05 m lower than NAVD88 (ellipsoid rather than geoid heights, by the look of it); the offset is constant across open ground, so the page adds 26.05 m to match the USGS DEM.
  • This is a surface model, not bare earth: the drone point cloud would be needed to classify ground returns and build a 6 cm terrain model.
  • Values in the readout are 1 m averages; the map tiles carry the full detail.

Next: LiDAR intensity

Intensity is the strength of each returned pulse. Near-infrared LiDAR is absorbed by water, so saturated peat, seeps and open water return weaker pulses than dry ground under the same vegetation. With the point cloud or an intensity raster, the fen's wettest cells could be mapped directly at decimetre scale and compared with the watershed model's predictions and the study-site outline. The layer list already has a slot for it.
