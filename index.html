Choosing the store size (station rain gauge)

Timing error: median days, model minus observed, for the autumn date each series first rises above its own median and the spring/summer date it first falls below it. Negative = model early. The first version of the model used a 100 mm store and wet up 42 days and dried out 25 days too early; every map and chart on this page now uses 250 mm.

The 50 cm and 100 cm probes step up in mid-December 2021: wet-season readings at 50 cm jump from about 0.30 to 0.45 m³/m³ and stay there, in a year that was not unusually wet. This looks like a sensor change, and it is why winters from 2022 on reach higher in the observed line. Using only 2010–2021 gives the same store size (250 mm: wet-up −1 day, dry-out −2 days, r 0.84).

Finding NWI wetlands within like land coverAUC

AUC here compares NWI wetland cells with non-wetland cells of the same NLCD 2021 land cover. Forest = deciduous, evergreen, mixed forest and woody wetland, scored with NWI forested/shrub wetlands. Open = shrub, grassland, pasture, crops and herbaceous wetland, scored with NWI emergent wetlands. Comparing across the whole watershed instead mostly measures forest against open land. 0.5 = no skill; for radar, above 0.5 means wetlands are brighter (or brighten more after rain).

What to check

  • Fen site. Median 1 m TWI , wetter than of the watershed. The wettest quarter of the site (TWI above ) is above the watershed's 95th percentile (). On the 16 m grid the fen ranks at the 94th percentile.
  • 1 m vs 16 m TWI. Switch between the two. At 1 m the floodplain breaks into thin threads of high TWI with drier ground between them. The NWI wetlands sit on that drier-looking ground, so 1 m TWI scores below chance (AUC 0.38). At 16 m the whole valley floor reads wet and AUC rises to 0.89. Averaging the 1 m TWI up to 16 m does not fix it; the DEM itself has to be coarser.
  • Hydroperiod. Mean days per year the model holds a cell at saturation (2003–2025, Stage IV rainfall, gridMET evapotranspiration, 250 mm root-zone store). With SSURGO soil the floodplain gravels drain fast and many NWI wetlands look dry. With uniform soil the map tracks 16 m TWI more closely. Open water (NHD) is masked and shows as grey relief.
  • Saturation calendar. The model never dries the whole fen: 3 of its 19 cells stay saturated every day. The rest is wettest from December to June and drains from July to November, most in dry years such as 2012, 2014 and 2017. Saturation peaks in May, about two months after the measured soil water at Salem, because water from the hillslopes reaches the valley floor late. NWI codes the fen B (saturated most of the year), which suggests groundwater keeps it wet when rain alone would not. Testing that needs water-table records at the fen.
  • NISAR radar. L-band backscatter is almost unrelated to TWI (rank correlation about 0) and much weaker on its own (AUC about 0.6 against 0.8–0.9 for TWI). Wetlands in forest are brighter in HH, as expected from trunk–water double bounce. NWI emergent wetlands brighten more after rain than other open ground, but there are only 327 such cells. Switch to NISAR HH wet − dry and look along the valley floors.
  • Large flat water bodies. The dark blue patches in the south-west and north-west are flat surfaces, likely ponds or reservoirs. Filling makes them perfectly flat, so they take the slope floor and the highest TWI.
  • Road crossings. In the close-up, short straight blue lines cut through the road embankment. These are the breach paths standing in for culverts.
